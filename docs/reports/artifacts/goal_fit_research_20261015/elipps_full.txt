Abstract
In undersampled inverse problems (such as sparse-view computed tomography), only a small number of measurements are collected, which reduces radiation exposure and acquisition time and cost, and can also address the inaccessibility of certain acquisition arrangements. Most learning-based methods for such problems require supervision in the form of fully sampled measurements and ground-truth images, which are costly or even infeasible to acquire. To overcome this issue, we propose Exact Learning for Inverse Problems from Partial Self-supervision (ELIPPS), an incomplete self-supervised training paradigm for undersampled inverse problems that requires neither ground-truth images nor fully sampled measurements. ELIPPS learns solely from incomplete forward measurements on a fixed incomplete supervision set. Our theory shows that when the data distribution is invariant under certain transformations, minimizing a masked empirical risk is equivalent to minimizing the full self-supervised risk, i.e. the risk against the complete, noise-free measurement. The equivalence is exact for arbitrary equivariant hypothesis classes and holds for signal-dependent noise such as the pre-log Poisson statistics of low-dose tomography, and for the log-transformed count model up to a quantifiable bias. When the underlying coverage condition is only approximately satisfied on a discrete grid, we give a stability estimate in terms of the associated frame constants and the irreducible error of the inverse problem. We realize ELIPPS for computed tomography by exploiting rotation and reflection invariance. In our experiments, ELIPPS substantially outperforms naive masked supervision and reaches the same order of accuracy as a reference model trained with full clean measurements.
Keywords: self-supervised learning, inverse problems, equivariance,
computed tomography, sparse-view reconstruction, conditional expectation.

1 Introduction
We study undersampled discrete inverse problems in which an unknown signal x∈ℝNx\in\mathbb{R}^{N} is observed through incomplete and noisy indirect measurements. Let A:ℝN→ℝMA:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M} denote the full forward operator. For I⊆{1,…,M}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\}, let PI:ℝM→ℝIP_{I}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{I} be the subsampling operator that selects the coordinates indexed by II, defined by PI​z=(zi)i∈IP_{I}z=(z_{i})_{i\in I}. Denote the subsampled forward operator by AI:=PI​AA_{I}:=P_{I}A. The undersampled inverse problem reads
yI=AI​x+ϵI,y_{I}=A_{I}x+\epsilon_{I},
(1.1)
where ϵI∈ℝI\epsilon_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I} is additive measurement noise. The objective is to recover the underlying image xx from the incompletely sampled measurements yI∈ℝIy_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}. Problem (1.1) is challenging due to both the ill-posedness of inverting AA and the incompleteness of the sampling process.
A prominent example for (1.1) is sparse-view computed tomography (CT), where the full forward operator AA corresponds to the Radon transform mapping an image to its projection measurements (sinogram). In practical CT systems, measurements are often incomplete or limited due to sparse-view acquisition, detector subsampling, or low-dose scanning, which are commonly employed to reduce radiation exposure and acquisition time. These constraints make the reconstruction problem severely ill-posed and can introduce strong artifacts when classical reconstruction methods are applied directly.
Self-supervised reconstruction:
Learning-based reconstruction methods have achieved remarkable performance for inverse problems, including (1.1). Most approaches are supervised and assume access to paired training data (yI,x)(y_{I},x). In practice, collecting xx is challenging; typically even during training one only has access to additional measurements yS=AS​x+ϵSy_{S}=A_{S}x+\epsilon_{S}, where S⊆{1,…,M}S\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\} is the supervision set and AS=PS​AA_{S}=P_{S}A. Inspired by self-supervised learning for denoising [1, 2, 3], several self-supervised strategies have been proposed that use only measurements of ySy_{S}. Self-supervised reconstruction methods can be roughly classified into: (i) methods with full self-supervision (AS=AA_{S}=A; at least in expectation), e.g., [4, 5, 6]; and (ii) methods with sparse self-supervision (AS=AIA_{S}=A_{I}), e.g., [7, 8, 9, 10]. The setting studied in this paper is a third class, partial self-supervision, in which AS=PS​AA_{S}=P_{S}A for a supervision set SS that is fixed across acquisitions. Full self-supervision requires additional scans, higher radiation dose, or sensing configurations unavailable at deployment. Training with sparse self-supervision suffers from incomplete coverage of the data distribution, which can induce non-uniqueness and reliance on implicit priors. A recurring mechanism in this literature is measurement splitting: the acquired data are divided
into an input part and a target part, and the network is trained to predict the latter from the
former. This is the idea behind self-supervised reconstruction in MRI [11] and its
analysis via variable-density resampling [6], and it is the
measurement-domain counterpart of training against a second noisy realization as in Noise2Noise
[12]. What distinguishes our setting from all of the
above is that the split is neither random nor varying: the index sets II and SS are fixed once and
for all by the acquisition.
A different line of research that leverages invariance is equivariant imaging (EI) [13, 14], which learns a reconstruction from the undersampled
measurements yIy_{I} by enforcing equivariance of the measurement–reconstruction pipeline through an
additional training penalty. Equivariant splitting [15] uses invariance of the signal distribution to
reinterpret a single measurement as a measurement of a transformed signal through a transformed
operator; see [16] for a comprehensive account of this field. Our setting differs from this line of work in two main respects. First, we assume partial self-supervision (1.2) on a fixed, deterministic supervision set SS, as opposed to unsupervised losses or random measurement splits, whose analysis requires sufficient diversity of the sampling patterns. Fixed SS reflects acquisition designs in which the retained measurement subset is determined by the hardware and identical across samples. Second, we do not promote equivariance through a penalty term but enforce it exactly by architectural symmetrization. Under a frame-type coverage condition we prove that the partially self-supervised risk and the full self-supervised risk are equivalent (Theorem 2.11), and we analyse when restricting the hypothesis class in this way costs nothing.
Partial self-supervision:
We propose an incomplete self-supervised setting for (1.1). For a fixed set S⊆{1,…,M}S\subseteq\left\{1,\dots,M\right\} we assume training data
(yI,yS)=(AI​x+ϵI,AS​x+ϵS),(y_{I},y_{S})=\bigl(A_{I}x+\epsilon_{I},\,A_{S}x+\epsilon_{S}\bigr),
(1.2)
i.e., pairs of undersampled measurements yIy_{I} on the fixed set II as in (1.1) together with an incomplete measurement AS​x+ϵSA_{S}x+\epsilon_{S} on the fixed set SS. Such partial self-supervision arises when the retained subset of measurements is fixed by the acquisition
design. In CT practice this is the case, for instance, when the set of projection angles is
restricted by hardware, acquisition-time or dose constraints, or when a fixed, deliberately chosen
part of the detector is read out at full resolution while the remainder is not. In all of these
cases fully sampled reference measurements are unavailable even at training time, so that neither
supervised learning nor full self-supervision is applicable.

As a concrete application, consider in-line industrial CT as illustrated in Figure 1.1. Here II and SS are separated by the acquisition itself rather than by a choice of dose. Source and
detector are fixed and the part is carried past them on a conveyor, so that the belt position takes
the role of the view direction and the position on the detector line that of the detector coordinate. Moving the detector away from the source leaves the number of detector elements
unchanged but narrows the fan they cover, so that the same line samples a smaller part of the fan
more finely. In production the detector sits close, giving a coarse measurement yIy_{I} over the whole
fan; for calibration or first-article inspection it is moved back once, giving a fine measurement
ySy_{S} over part of it. Both sets are fixed by the machine settings and neither contains the other.
Parts that pass through both settings provide the training pairs (1.2); at deployment
only yIy_{I} exists. Parts arrive in arbitrary in-plane orientation, so that the invariance of the
signal distribution is a property of the application rather than one enforced by augmentation.
Learning under partial self-supervision is underdetermined: a regression loss that penalizes
discrepancies only on the observed subset defined by PSP_{S} leaves the other coordinates
unconstrained. As a result, models trained with naive self-supervision produce reconstructions that
are locally consistent but globally inconsistent or artifact-prone. While [17] showed
that noise-free inpainting (A=IdA=\operatorname{Id} and ϵI=0\epsilon_{I}=0) can be solved from incomplete supervision
(1.2), by exploiting equivariance of the restoration map that fills in the missing
data, the general case of incomplete, indirect observations with noise remains open. The step from
that setting to the present one is not merely one of generality. With A=IdA=\operatorname{Id} the measurement and
image domains coincide and the initial reconstruction is the identity, so that neither the interplay
between transformations of the two domains nor the question of whether the admitted class of
image-domain restoration maps is rich enough arises. Moreover, with AA ill-posed, noise is a major source of difficulty: the amplification of small
perturbations makes the inverse problem challenging, and a noise-free model would miss this issue. The
target of the masked regression is then no longer the clean signal: without noise the conditional
expectation coincides with XX wherever the observation determines it, with noise it does not.
Main contributions:
We propose ELIPPS for (1.1) with incomplete self-supervised data (1.2). Our approach leverages invariance properties of the signal class together with intertwining relations of the forward map AA to enforce equivariance of the learned reconstruction with respect to known transformations. The key result shows that, over an equivariant function class, minimizing the partially
self-supervised risk 𝔼⁡[∥PS​g​(YI)−YS∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}\,g(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr] is equivalent to minimizing the full self-supervised risk 𝔼⁡[∥g⁡(YI)−A​X∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert g(Y_{I})-AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]. This enables globally consistent learning from partial self-supervision. We instantiate the approach for CT using detector upscaling (subsampling along the detector axis) and sparse-view reconstruction (subsampling along the angular axis).
Specific results:
(i) A framework built on invariances of the signal class and intertwining relations of the forward operator, with a proof that masked regression over an equivariant class is equivalent to the full self-supervised risk (Theorem 2.11). ((ii) A characterization of the admissible supervision sets: a coverage condition on the transformed copies of SS is necessary as well as sufficient for the two risks to be proportional, and it bounds how small SS can be by the number of transformations and by the stabilizers of the action (Propositions 2.14 and 2.15). (iii) A network construction that enforces equivariance by design and applies to any modality whose
forward operator intertwines with the transformations (Theorem 2.20), with a proof that
this restriction does not change the minimizer (Theorem 2.23,
Corollary 2.28), a counterexample showing which property of the initial
reconstruction cannot be dropped (Remark 2.27), and an exact expression for what is lost when that property fails (Proposition 2.25).
(iv) An exactness result for arbitrary equivariant hypothesis classes, which separates the correctness of the training criterion from the expressivity of the architecture (Corollary 2.13), and a stability estimate for an only approximately satisfied coverage condition (Theorem 2.17). (v) The combination of these results into the statement used in practice, namely that the minimizer over the trained architecture class is the conditional expectation (Corollary 2.29) and that the learned reconstruction agrees with the minimum mean squared error estimator up to ker⁡(A)\ker(A) (Corollary 2.30), together with a practical training objective and implementation (Section 2.5, equation (2.5)), realized for CT under low-dose Poisson noise and severe undersampling, reaching the accuracy of a reference model trained with full clean measurements (Section 3, Table 3.1). (vi) An ablation on the role of the symmetry of the training distribution (Section 3.7, Table 3.2).

2 Incomplete Self-Supervised Learning
In this section we introduce and analyze Exact Learning for Inverse Problems from Partial
Self-supervision (ELIPPS) for solving (1.1) using partial self-supervision
(1.2). We adopt a probabilistic setting on a common probability space
(Ω,ℱ,ℙ)(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}): the unknown image xx is the realization of a random vector (RV)
XX with unknown distribution; the observed subsampled measurements are YI=AI​X+ϵIY_{I}=A_{I}X+\epsilon_{I}
and the supervision measurement is YS=AS​X+ϵSY_{S}=A_{S}X+\epsilon_{S}. All expectations are taken with
respect to the joint distribution induced by (1.1) and (1.2). For RVs Y,ZY,Z we write Y=dZY\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z for equality in distribution and Y=ZY=Z for
almost sure equality (unless indicated otherwise, almost surely always refers to
ℙ\mathbb{P}).
Throughout, we write ℝI\mathbb{R}^{I} for the coordinate space indexed by II and identify
ℝI≃ℝI×{0}⊆ℝM\mathbb{R}^{I}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{I}\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{M}, so that the orthogonal
projector PIP_{I} acts as a binary masking operator on ℝM\mathbb{R}^{M}, zeroing out the coordinates
outside II; analogously for SS.
2.1 Preliminaries
Let T:ℝM→ℝMT:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M} be an invertible linear map. The first key ingredient of ELIPPS is invariance of the underlying signal class.
Definition 2.1 (TT-invariant RV).
Let T:ℝM→ℝMT:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M} be an invertible linear map. A RV YY on ℝM\mathbb{R}^{M} is said to be invariant with respect to TT (or TT-invariant, in distribution) if
Y=dT⁡(Y)Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}T(Y).
Note that Y=dT⁡(Y)Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}T(Y) is much weaker than Y=T⁡(Y)Y=T(Y). Consider a
distribution of images that is invariant under a rotation TT by 90∘90^{\circ}, for instance because
every orientation of every image occurs with the same probability. Then Y=dT⁡(Y)Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}T(Y),
whereas T⁡(Y)=YT(Y)=Y would require every single image to be unchanged by the rotation, which holds
only for rotationally symmetric images. We write πY\pi_{Y} for the law of YY, i.e. πY​(E)=ℙ⁡(Y∈E)\pi_{Y}(E)=\mathbb{P}(Y\in E) for Borel sets E⊆ℝME\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{M}. In this notation the
TT-invariance of YY reads πT⁡(Y)=πY\pi_{T(Y)}=\pi_{Y}, or equivalently
ℙ⁡(Y∈E)=ℙ⁡(T⁡(Y)∈E)\mathbb{P}(Y\in E)=\mathbb{P}(T(Y)\in E) for all EE Borel.
Definition 2.2 (Joint TT-invariance).
Let Y,ZY,Z be RVs on ℝM\mathbb{R}^{M}. The pair (Z,Y)(Z,Y) is said to be jointly TT-invariant in distribution if (Z,Y)=d(T⁡(Z),T⁡(Y))(Z,Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(T(Z),T(Y)).
Besides invariance of the of RVs, we also use invariance of the sampling set II.
Definition 2.3 (TT-invariant subsampling).
We say that the sampling set I⊆{1,…,M}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\} is TT-invariant if
PI​T=T​PIP_{I}T=TP_{I}.
Remark 2.4.
The commutation PI​T=T​PIP_{I}T=TP_{I} holds precisely when TT is block-diagonal with respect to
ℝM=ℝI×ℝJ\mathbb{R}^{M}=\mathbb{R}^{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{J} with J={1,…,M}∖IJ=\{1,\dots,M\}\setminus I; that is,
when TT maps the observed coordinates among themselves and likewise the unobserved ones. For a
permutation TT this means that II is a union of cycles of TT, and then so is JJ automatically;
this is how PI​T=T​PIP_{I}T=TP_{I} is verified in Section 3. For the main results it
suffices to assume joint invariance directly, without the commutativity. We state the latter
explicitly because it is what the sufficient condition of Lemma 2.5 and the
sample-wise identity of Proposition 2.14 use.
Lemma 2.5 (Sufficient conditions for joint invariance).
Let YY be a RV on ℝM\mathbb{R}^{M}, let I⊆{1,…,M}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\}, and let

TT-invariant and that II is a TT-invariant sampling set. Then the pair (YI,Y)(Y_{I},Y) is jointly

Proof.

2.2 Measurement Self-supervision
We target the image restoration problem (1.1) of reconstructing a signal or image X∈ℝNX\in\mathbb{R}^{N} from incomplete, noisy, indirect observations. We consider a random setting with deterministic design and partial self-supervision (1.2). The method and main results are derived under the following assumptions.
Condition 2.9.
Assume the following hold:
(A1)
A:ℝN→ℝMA\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M} is a fixed potentially nonlinear forward map.
(A2)
I,S⊆{1,…,M}I,S\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\} are fixed subsampling (input) and supervision index sets.
(A3)
The data are generated according to the partial self-supervision model (1.2).
(A4)
XX, ϵI\epsilon_{I} and ϵS\epsilon_{S} are square-integrable, and 𝔼[ϵS∣X,ϵI]=0\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_{S}\mid X,\epsilon_{I}]=0.
(A5)
𝒯=(Tℓ)ℓ=1L\mathcal{T}=(T_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{L} is a finite group of invertible linear operators on ℝM\mathbb{R}^{M}, of order LL.
(A6)
For every ℓ\ell, the pair (YI,A​X)(Y_{I},AX) is jointly TℓT_{\ell}-invariant.
(A7)
There exists c>0c>0 such that for all v∈ℝM:∑ℓ=1L‖PS​Tℓ​v‖22=c​‖v‖22v\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\colon\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\|P_{S}\,T_{\ell}v\|_{2}^{2}=c\,\|v\|_{2}^{2}.
Remark 2.10 (On the noise assumption (A4)).
The usual assumption in this context, that ϵS\epsilon_{S} be zero-mean and independent of
(X,ϵI)(X,\epsilon_{I}), implies (A4), but not conversely. In particular, (A4) requires the
noise neither to be additive nor signal-independent nor identically distributed across
coordinates, and it imposes no assumption whatsoever on the joint law of XX and ϵI\epsilon_{I}.
It is satisfied whenever the supervision measurement is an unbiased acquisition that is
conditionally independent of the input acquisition given the signal: if
𝔼⁡[YS∣X]=AS​X\mathbb{E}[Y_{S}\mid X]=A_{S}X and YSY_{S} is conditionally independent of ϵI\epsilon_{I} given
XX, then the latter gives 𝔼[YS∣X,ϵI]=𝔼[YS∣X]\mathbb{E}[Y_{S}\mid X,\epsilon_{I}]=\mathbb{E}[Y_{S}\mid X], whence
𝔼[ϵS∣X,ϵI]=𝔼[YS−ASX∣X,ϵI]=𝔼[YS∣X]−ASX=0\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_{S}\mid X,\epsilon_{I}]=\mathbb{E}[Y_{S}-A_{S}X\mid X,\epsilon_{I}]=\mathbb{E}[Y_{S}\mid X]-A_{S}X=0.
This covers signal-dependent noise, in particular Poisson statistics, and requires no Gaussian
approximation. It does require the supervision measurement to be unbiased, which is not
automatic: if the sinogram is formed from photon counts by a log transform, a systematic bias
enters. Remark 2.12 below shows that such a bias propagates in a controlled way.
Note that Condition (A4) implies that the supervision measurement must not
reuse the noise of the input measurement. Physically this leaves two options: either two
conditionally independent acquisitions of the same object, in the spirit of Noise2Noise
[12], or a single acquisition split into two independent parts, achieved for
photon counts by Poisson thinning at unchanged total dose. A disjoint design
I∩S=∅I\cap S=\emptyset would also suffice, but (A7) forces SS to meet every orbit
while PI​Tℓ=Tℓ​PIP_{I}T_{\ell}=T_{\ell}P_{I} forces II to be a union of orbits, so the two sets must
overlap; see Section 3.
Throughout, every minimization over a class of maps is understood to range over those gg in the class for which g⁡(YI)g(Y_{I}) is square-integrable. Identities between minimizers are understood in the usual
L2L^{2} sense: the arg​min\argmin is the set of minimizers of the stated risk, all of whose elements
agree πYI\pi_{Y_{I}}-almost everywhere, and an equation of the form
𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]=arg​min⁡(⋅)\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}]=\argmin(\cdot) asserts that every minimizer gg satisfies
g⁡(YI)=𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]g(Y_{I})=\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}] almost surely. Whether the minimum is attained is addressed by
Lemma 2.8 and, for the constrained classes of Sections 2.3
and 2.4, by Corollary 2.28.
Theorem 2.11 (Exact learning from partial self-supervision).
Suppose Condition 2.9 holds. Then, over the equivariant function class Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T}), minimizing the partially self-supervised noisy-target risk recovers the clean conditional expectation:
𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]=arg​ming∈Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)⁡𝔼​[∥PS​g​(YI)−YS∥22].\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}]=\argmin_{g\in\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T})}\;\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}\,g(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr].
Proof.
Let Z=𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]Z=\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}]; by Lemmas 2.7 and 2.8 (applied with Y=A​XY=AX) it is attained by a map in Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T}). For any square-integrable g:ℝM→ℝMg:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}, expand the noisy-target risk:
𝔼⁡[∥PS​g​(YI)−YS∥22]=𝔼⁡[∥PS​g​(YI)−PS​A​X∥22]+𝔼⁡[∥ϵS∥22]−2​𝔼​[⟨PS​(g⁡(YI)−A​X),ϵS⟩].\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}g(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}g(Y_{I})-P_{S}AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert\epsilon_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]-2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\langle P_{S}(g(Y_{I})-AX),\epsilon_{S}\rangle\right].
Since g⁡(YI)−A​Xg(Y_{I})-AX is σ⁡(X,ϵI)\sigma(X,\epsilon_{I})-measurable, conditioning on (X,ϵI)(X,\epsilon_{I}) and using (A4) shows that the cross term vanishes. Since 𝔼⁡[∥ϵS∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert\epsilon_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr] does not depend on gg, minimizing the masked noisy risk is equivalent to minimizing the masked clean risk 𝔼⁡[∥PS​g​(YI)−PS​A​X∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}g(Y_{I})-P_{S}AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr].
Let gg be 𝒯\mathcal{T}-equivariant and define R=g⁡(YI)−A​XR=g(Y_{I})-AX. By equivariance, g⁡(Tℓ​YI)−Tℓ​A​X=Tℓ​Rg(T_{\ell}Y_{I})-T_{\ell}AX=T_{\ell}R; by joint invariance (Condition (A6)), 𝔼⁡[∥PS​R∥22]=𝔼⁡[∥PS​Tℓ​R∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}R\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}T_{\ell}R\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr] for all ℓ\ell. Averaging over ℓ\ell and using Condition (A7) yields
𝔼⁡[∥PS​g​(YI)−PS​A​X∥22]=1L​𝔼​[∑ℓ=1L‖PS​Tℓ​R‖22]=cL​𝔼​[∥R∥22]=cL​𝔼​[∥g⁡(YI)−A​X∥22].\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}g(Y_{I})-P_{S}AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\frac{1}{L}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\|P_{S}T_{\ell}R\|_{2}^{2}\right]=\frac{c}{L}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert R\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\frac{c}{L}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert g(Y_{I})-AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr].
Therefore, within Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T}), the masked clean risk is strictly proportional to the full clean risk, and both are minimized by Z=𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]Z=\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}].
∎
Remark 2.12 (Biased supervision).
Suppose that instead of (A4) the supervision noise satisfies
𝔼[ϵS∣X,ϵI]=PSβ(X)\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_{S}\mid X,\epsilon_{I}]=P_{S}\,\beta(X) for a measurable bias map
β:ℝN→ℝM\beta\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}, and that the pair (YI,A​X+β⁡(X))(Y_{I},AX+\beta(X)) is jointly
TℓT_{\ell}-invariant for every ℓ\ell (which holds, for instance, when β\beta acts
coordinatewise on A​xAx and the TℓT_{\ell} are coordinate permutations). Writing
ϵS=PS​β​(X)+η\epsilon_{S}=P_{S}\beta(X)+\eta with 𝔼[η∣X,ϵI]=0\mathbb{E}[\eta\mid X,\epsilon_{I}]=0 and repeating the
proof of Theorem 2.11 with A​XAX replaced by A​X+β⁡(X)AX+\beta(X) gives
arg​ming∈Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)⁡𝔼​[∥PS​g​(YI)−YS∥22]=𝔼⁡[AX+β⁡(X)|YI].\argmin_{g\in\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T})}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}g(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[AX+\beta(X)\,\big|\,Y_{I}\bigr].
The minimizer of the partially self-supervised risk is therefore again a conditional
expectation, now of the biased quantity A​X+β⁡(X)AX+\beta(X) instead of A​XAX: the learned predictor
inherits the bias of the supervision measurement and nothing else. Its deviation from
𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}] is 𝔼⁡[β⁡(X)∣YI]\mathbb{E}[\beta(X)\mid Y_{I}], whose L2L^{2} norm is at most
‖β⁡(X)‖L2\|\beta(X)\|_{L^{2}} because conditional expectation is an L2L^{2} contraction. The latter can be
estimated from a concrete acquisition model; see Section 3.
The proof gives more than the statement of Theorem 2.11: on the whole equivariant class the two risks differ only by a positive factor and an additive constant, which yields an exactness statement independent of any modelling assumption.
Corollary 2.13 (Exactness within arbitrary equivariant subclasses).
Suppose Condition 2.9 holds and let 𝒞⊆Equi⁡(ℝM;𝒯)\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T}) be an arbitrary nonempty class of
square-integrable equivariant maps. Then
arg​ming∈𝒞⁡𝔼​[∥PS​g​(YI)−YS∥22]=arg​ming∈𝒞⁡𝔼​[∥g⁡(YI)−𝔼⁡[AX∣YI]∥22],\argmin_{g\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}\,g(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\argmin_{g\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert g(Y_{I})-\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}]\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr],
i.e. training on partially self-supervised, noisy data over 𝒞\mathcal{C} returns the best
L2L^{2}-approximation of 𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}] available in 𝒞\mathcal{C}.
Proof.
Write Z=𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]Z=\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}]. By the two displays in the proof of Theorem 2.11,
every g∈Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)g\in\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T}) satisfies
𝔼⁡[∥PS​g​(YI)−YS∥22]=cL​𝔼​[∥g⁡(YI)−A​X∥22]+𝔼⁡[∥ϵS∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}g(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\frac{c}{L}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert g(Y_{I})-AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert\epsilon_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]. Since g⁡(YI)g(Y_{I}) is σ⁡(YI)\sigma(Y_{I})-measurable and 𝔼⁡[A​X−Z∣YI]=0\mathbb{E}[AX-Z\mid Y_{I}]=0, the Pythagoras
identity for conditional expectations gives 𝔼⁡[∥g⁡(YI)−A​X∥22]=𝔼⁡[∥g⁡(YI)−Z∥22]+𝔼⁡[∥A​X−Z∥22]\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert g(Y_{I})-AX\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert g(Y_{I})-Z\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert AX-Z\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr], and the last term does not depend on gg. The two objectives therefore differ by the positive
factor c/Lc/L and an additive constant, which do not change the set of minimizers.
∎
Corollary 2.13 makes ELIPPS robust with respect to modelling choices. Whatever the hypothesis class, minimizing the partially self-supervised risk over an equivariant class is therefore always equivalent to minimizing the full self-supervised risk over the same class. Assumptions beyond Condition 2.9, in particular those of Section 2.4, are never needed for the correctness of the training objective; they only decide whether 𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}] lies in the class.
Theorem 2.11 is a statement about expectations. When the training set is closed under the group action, a purely deterministic statement is available as well: on the orbit of a single
sample, the masked loss and the full unmasked loss coincide.
Proposition 2.14 (Orbit identity).
Let 𝒯\mathcal{T} satisfy (A7), let PI​Tℓ=Tℓ​PIP_{I}T_{\ell}=T_{\ell}P_{I} for every
ℓ\ell, and let g∈Equi⁡(ℝM,𝒯)g\in\Equi(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathcal{T}). For a measurement vector
y∈ℝMy\in\mathbb{R}^{M} put yI=PI​yy_{I}=P_{I}y, and consider the orbit of yy under 𝒯\mathcal{T} with the associated
input and supervision data yI(ℓ)=PI​Tℓ​yy_{I}^{(\ell)}=P_{I}T_{\ell}y and yS(ℓ)=PS​Tℓ​yy_{S}^{(\ell)}=P_{S}T_{\ell}y. Then
∑ℓ=1L‖PS​g​(yI(ℓ))−yS(ℓ)‖22=c​‖g⁡(yI)−y‖22.\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\bigl\|P_{S}\,g\bigl(y_{I}^{(\ell)}\bigr)-y_{S}^{(\ell)}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;c\,\bigl\|g(y_{I})-y\bigr\|_{2}^{2}.
Proof.
Since PIP_{I} commutes with TℓT_{\ell} we have yI(ℓ)=Tℓ​PI​y=Tℓ​yIy_{I}^{(\ell)}=T_{\ell}P_{I}y=T_{\ell}y_{I}, and
equivariance of gg gives g⁡(yI(ℓ))=Tℓ​g​(yI)g(y_{I}^{(\ell)})=T_{\ell}\,g(y_{I}). Hence
PS​g​(yI(ℓ))−yS(ℓ)=PS​Tℓ​vP_{S}g(y_{I}^{(\ell)})-y_{S}^{(\ell)}=P_{S}T_{\ell}v with v=g⁡(yI)−yv=g(y_{I})-y, and summing over ℓ\ell
and using (A7) yields ∑ℓ‖PS​Tℓ​v‖22=c​‖v‖22\sum_{\ell}\|P_{S}T_{\ell}v\|_{2}^{2}=c\|v\|_{2}^{2}.
∎
The identity in Proposition 2.14 is exact and finite: it involves no probability distribution, no invariance
assumption on the data and no expectation. If the training set is closed under the group
action, the empirical masked risk over the orbit-closed set therefore equals cc times the
empirical full risk over the original samples, for every equivariant candidate.
Enlarging the training set by the group is thus not a way of obtaining more data but the
finite realization of the coverage condition (A7), which the next proposition
turns into a characterization. Theorem 2.11 is the expectation version of the same
mechanism. There the noise contributes a cross term that vanishes only in the mean, which is
precisely where (A4) and the joint invariance (A6) enter.
Proposition 2.15 (Characterization of the coverage condition).
Let 𝒯=(Tℓ)ℓ=1L\mathcal{T}=(T_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{L} be a finite set of invertible linear maps, not necessarily a
group, and let S⊆{1,…,M}S\subseteq\{1,\dots,M\}. Then (A7) holds, that is
∑ℓ∥PS​Tℓ​v∥22=c​∥v∥22\sum_{\ell}\lVert P_{S}T_{\ell}v\rVert_{2}^{2}=c\,\lVert v\rVert_{2}^{2} for every v∈ℝMv\in\mathbb{R}^{M}, if and
only if
∑ℓ=1LTℓ⊤​PS​Tℓ=c​Id.\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}T_{\ell}^{\!\top}P_{S}\,T_{\ell}=c\,\operatorname{Id}.
(2.1)
Proof.
For every vv we have
∑ℓ∥PS​Tℓ​v∥22=∑ℓ⟨Tℓ⊤​PS⊤​PS​Tℓ​v,v⟩=⟨∑ℓTℓ⊤​PS​Tℓ​v,v⟩\sum_{\ell}\lVert P_{S}T_{\ell}v\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\ell}\langle T_{\ell}^{\!\top}P_{S}^{\!\top}P_{S}T_{\ell}\,v,v\rangle=\langle\sum_{\ell}T_{\ell}^{\!\top}P_{S}T_{\ell}\,v,v\rangle,
because PS⊤​PS=PSP_{S}^{\!\top}P_{S}=P_{S} for an orthogonal projection. A symmetric matrix is determined
by its quadratic form, so this equals c​∥v∥22c\lVert v\rVert_{2}^{2} for all vv if and only if
(2.1) holds.
∎
In the language of frame theory, (2.1) states that the rows of the maps
(PS​Tℓ)ℓ=1L(P_{S}T_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{L} form a tight frame of ℝM\mathbb{R}^{M} with frame constant cc. For
orthogonal TℓT_{\ell}, taking traces gives |S|=c​M/L\lvert S\rvert=c\,M/L, which is the quantitative content
of the condition: the supervision set must contain a fraction c/Lc/L of all measurements, so the achievable compression is bounded by LL. Under Condition 2.9, where 𝒯\mathcal{T} is a group, LL is its order; for the dihedral group D4D_{4} used in Section 3 this is a factor of eight.
Whether c=1c=1 is attainable depends on the action. If the TℓT_{\ell} are permutations and a
coordinate ii is fixed by kk of them, then every coordinate that SS reaches in the same orbit
contributes kk to the multiplicity of ii, so tightness forces c≥kc\geq k and hence
|S|≥k​M/L\lvert S\rvert\geq k\,M/L. Only a free action, in which no coordinate is fixed by a nontrivial group
element, admits c=1c=1 and |S|=M/L\lvert S\rvert=M/L. On a discrete grid the reflection axes of D4D_{4}
produce fixed coordinates; the weighted formulation of Remark 2.16 removes the
obstruction by counting them with the reciprocal of their multiplicity.
Conversely, the condition tells us how to choose SS: as a fundamental domain of the group action,
which is a design decision rather than something found in the data.

Condition (A7) requires the transformed supervision sets to cover the measurement
domain exactly. On a discrete grid this can fail by a small amount. The following stability estimate shows that the conclusion of Theorem 2.11 survives on the part of the measurement domain that is covered at all, no matter how unevenly.

The averaging in (2.3) is the classical Reynolds operator, and its use to

Definition 2.21 (Right-inverse and least-squares right-inverse).
Let A:ℝN→ℝMA\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M} be a linear operator and Pran(A):ℝM→ℝMP_{\range(A)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M} the orthogonal projection onto the range of AA. Remark 2.26 (What the criterion targets).
All results of this section identify the conditional expectation, that is, the minimum mean squared

Remark 2.31 (The null space is not identified by the risk alone).
Corollary 2.30 is sharp: the training risk depends on A​f∘BIAf\circ B_{I} only, so no criterion of this form determines the component in ker⁡(A)\ker(A). For the discretized Radon transform of Section 3, which maps 3612=130 321361^{2}=130\,321 pixels to 128×361=46 208128\times 361=46\,208 measurements, the image-domain statement therefore holds only modulo ker⁡(A)\ker(A). What constrains that component in practice is the hypothesis class: the equivariance of fθf_{\theta}, the choice of BIB_{I} and the implicit bias of the network. Combining the sensing theorems of [18, 13] with the present setting is a natural next step.
Remark 2.32 (Biased supervision within the factorized class).
For the constrained class Equi⁡(A,BI,𝒮)\Equi(A,B_{I};\mathcal{S}), Remark 2.12 needs one qualification: its elements take values in ran(A)\range(A), whereas 𝔼⁡[A​X+β⁡(X)∣YI]\mathbb{E}[AX+\beta(X)\mid Y_{I}] need not, so the attainability step of Corollary 2.29 may fail. Consistency is unaffected, since by Corollary 2.13 training returns the L2L^{2}-projection of the shifted target onto the realized class. The qualification is vacuous when ran(A)=ℝM\range(A)=\mathbb{R}^{M}, the expected situation in Section 3.
Remark 2.33 (Role of Condition 2.22).
The two conditions play different roles. Condition 2.9 concerns the training criterion: by Corollary 2.13 it makes the partially self-supervised risk rank equivariant candidates exactly as the full self-supervised risk does, for every hypothesis class. Condition 2.22 concerns the hypothesis class: it keeps the optimum from being excluded by the factorized architecture. If it is violated, training still returns the best approximation of 𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}] within the realized class, so misspecification causes an approximation error, never an inconsistent target. The experiments in Section 3 operate in that regime.

2.5 Summary of the method
Suppose the image model satisfies Conditions 2.9 and 2.22 with transforms satisfying Condition 2.18, and let (Φθ)θ∈Θ(\Phi_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta} be any image-space network. According to Theorem 2.20 the functions fθ=1L​∑ℓSℓ−1∘Φθ∘Sℓf_{\theta}=\tfrac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell}S_{\ell}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{\theta}\circ S_{\ell} and gθ=A∘fθ∘Bg_{\theta}=A\circ f_{\theta}\circ B are 𝒮\mathcal{S}- and 𝒯\mathcal{T}-equivariant, respectively. By Theorem 2.23 and Corollary 2.28 this restriction costs nothing when BB is a least-squares right-inverse of PI∘AP_{I}\circ A, and Corollary 2.29 gives the statement used in practice: the minimizer of the partially self-supervised noisy risk over this class is 𝔼⁡[A​X∣YI]\mathbb{E}[AX\mid Y_{I}], and by Corollary 2.30 the reconstruction fθ∘Bf_{\theta}\circ B agrees with the minimum mean squared error estimator up to ker⁡(A)\ker(A). For an approximate inverse such as the FBP, Remark 2.33 shows that only the expressivity of the class is affected, not the correctness of the criterion. The training itself uses only the partial self-supervision data (1.2), through
ℒ𝒯​(θ)=𝔼⁡[∥PS∘gθ​(YI)−YS∥22]=𝔼⁡[∥AS∘fθ∘B⁡(YI)−YS∥22].\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert P_{S}\circ g_{\theta}(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\lVert A_{S}\circ f_{\theta}\circ B(Y_{I})-Y_{S}\rVert_{2}^{2}\bigr].
(2.5)
At test time, the reconstruction is simply obtained by the learned reconstruction maps fθ∘Bf_{\theta}\circ B.
3 Experiments
This section evaluates ELIPPS under severe partial self-supervision and low-dose noise. We consider two instantiations of the undersampled inverse problem (1.1) in CT, which differ only in the axis along which the sinogram is subsampled:
•
Detector Upscaling: only a subset of detector bins is observed; the goal is to recover the full-resolution measurement and a corresponding reconstruction.
•
Sparse-View Reconstruction: only a subset of projection angles is observed; the goal is to recover a high-quality reconstruction from angularly undersampled measurements.
In both settings, we instantiate the transformation families 𝒯\mathcal{T} and 𝒮\mathcal{S} of Section 2.2 via the eight symmetries of the dihedral group
D4={Id,r,r2,r3,σ,r​σ,r2​σ,r3​σ},D_{4}=\{\operatorname{Id},\,r,\,r^{2},\,r^{3},\,\sigma,\,r\sigma,\,r^{2}\sigma,\,r^{3}\sigma\},
(3.1)
where rr is the rotation by 90∘90^{\circ} and σ\sigma the reflection about the vertical image axis.
The group D4D_{4} acts on the image domain by rotations and reflections of the pixel grid and, via the intertwining relation with the Radon transform, on the measurement domain by permutations of the sinogram coordinates. This makes the abstract framework of Section 2.2 fully explicit: we verify Conditions 2.9 and 2.18 for this instantiation below.
3.1 Dataset and Acquisition Model
We use a 1%1\% subset of the LoDoPaB dataset [23], consisting of 358358 training and 3535 test images. Unless stated otherwise (see the ablation in Section 3.7), the training set is augmented by the full D4D_{4} group, yielding an 8×8\times larger training set whose empirical distribution is D4D_{4}-invariant by construction.
All images are resized to 361×361361\times 361 pixels and multiplied by a circular disk mask restricting the reconstruction to the field of view. Here 361361 is the side length of the

Augmentation enforces (A6) rather than verifying it, and the two levels at which this matters should be separated. On the level of distributions, writing πX\pi_{X} for the law of the images in the data set and π¯X:=18​∑ℓ=18πSℓ​(X)\bar{\pi}_{X}:=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\ell=1}^{8}\pi_{S_{\ell}(X)} for its D4D_{4}-symmetrization,

not coincide with the conditional expectation under πX\pi_{X}; evaluation on the original test images is
therefore a generalization test beyond the invariance assumption rather than an instance of it.
On the level of the training objective, however, no such gap arises. Since the augmented training
set is closed under the group action, Proposition 2.14 applies to each original image
separately: for every equivariant candidate, the masked loss summed over the eight augmented copies
of an image equals cc times the full, unmasked loss on that image. The empirical objective
being minimized is thus exactly the full self-supervised empirical objective on the 358358 original
images, up to the noise cross term that vanishes in the mean. Augmentation is therefore not a distortion of the training target but the finite mechanism that realizes the coverage condition; what it changes is the population to which the estimator is optimal.
Relation to the noise model:
The conditional independence of the two acquisitions is what brings the setup close to (A4); no Gaussian approximation of the Poisson statistics is used anywhere in the analysis.
What is satisfied only approximately is Condition 2.22: the FBP-based initial
reconstruction is not a least-squares right-inverse of PI∘AP_{I}\circ A (Remark 2.27),
and the noise decomposition 2.22.2 holds exactly for i.i.d. Gaussian

hypothesis class, in which case training returns its best approximation therein. Together with the
coverage defect quantified above, the experiments therefore serve as a robustness study for
precisely those quantities that the theory identifies as the sources of approximation error:
ELIPPS reaches, despite both violations, the same order of accuracy as the supervised reference model; the precise reading of that comparison is discussed in Section 3.6.
3.4 Implementation Details
All learned models use a U-Net backbone Φθ\Phi_{\theta} with base width 3232 and three down-/up-sampling stages. We train with Adam (learning rate 10−410^{-4}), gradient clipping ‖∇θℒ‖2≤1\|\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}\|_{2}\leq 1, and batch size 88. Training runs for 10001000 epochs (20002000 for the ablation of Section 3.7), and

3.7 Ablation Study: Effect of D4D_{4} Data Augmentation
The theory of Section 2.2 requires the D4D_{4}-invariance of the data distribution, which we enforced above by explicit D4D_{4} augmentation. To assess the role of this augmentation, we repeat the training on the same 1%1\% LoDoPaB subset without D4D_{4} augmentation. In this setting, the invariance assumption (A6) is no longer enforced exactly, and the network must rely on the architectural equivariance and the physics-driven loss alone to generalize across unobserved regions. Training runs for 20002000 epochs and test evaluation is performed every 1010 epochs. The budget therefore differs from the 10001000 epochs used for the main results, so the two tables are not a like-for-like comparison; the ablation should be read as evidence that the architectural prior remains effective without symmetry-balanced data, not as a measurement of the size of the effect.
Performance remains stable (Table 3.2): relative to the D4D_{4}-augmented setting ELIPPS loses 0.520.52 dB for detector upscaling and 0.810.81 dB for sparse-view reconstruction, with gaps to the reference of 0.190.19 and 0.440.44 dB, while still outperforming the naive baseline by a wide margin. Architectural equivariance therefore acts as a regularizer even when the training data are not symmetry-balanced.
Figures 3.5 and 3.6 qualify these numbers. Without augmentation the ELIPPS curves peak at roughly 900900 to 12001200 epochs and then decline slowly, whereas the reference stays flat, so the fixed budget of 20002000 epochs lies past the maximum and part of the gap above follows from the budget rather than from the method. We report the final-epoch value nonetheless, since with no validation set selecting the peak would be selection on the test set. We do not interpret the decline: these are single runs on 358358 images, and ordinary overfitting is as plausible as any consequence of the violated (A6).

3.8 Scope of the experimental study
Section 3 establishes a principle rather than a benchmark ranking: a reconstruction network can be trained from a fixed, incomplete supervision set alone and still reach the accuracy of a model trained with full clean measurements. The two instantiations were chosen so that this effect is visible rather than entangled with architecture search, and together with the reference points that bracket the setting they show that supervision on one eighth of the sinogram suffices.
We are not aware of another method that learns from partial self-supervision in the sense of
(1.2), that is, from a supervision set that is fixed across acquisitions. Existing
approaches either require ground-truth images or fully sampled measurements, or they learn from
several distinct measurement operators [24], or from the
undersampled measurement yIy_{I} alone, without any supervision set; the latter, including equivariant
imaging and equivariant splitting, operate under the opposite requirement on the symmetry group
discussed in Remark 2.19 and would, in the regime considered here, be deprived of the
mechanism they rely on. A comparison with either family therefore answers a different question. The closest relative is [17], which the present framework generalizes from direct, noise-free observation to indirect, noisy and undersampled measurements.
Two aspects delimit what these numbers support. The study uses 358358 training and 3535 test images from a 1%1\% subset of LoDoPaB with one run per configuration, which fixes the resolution at which the comparison is meaningful; and the input and supervision sinograms come from two conditionally independent Poisson realizations, costing between 1.251.25 and 22 times the deployment scan depending on the task, so the protocol demonstrates the learning principle rather than a dose-neutral single-scan implementation, for which Poisson thinning is the natural variant.
Two properties of the construction are design principles rather than artefacts of this data set, and carry over to any modality whose forward operator intertwines with a finite symmetry group. The supervision set enters through its orbit, so what makes learning from one eighth of the measurements possible is the covering property of Proposition 2.15; SS is chosen by design and, by the same proposition, cannot be smaller, the eighth being attainable because the weighted mask makes the action effectively free. And since the training set is closed under the group action, Proposition 2.14 applies sample by sample: the masked loss minimized here is, for every equivariant candidate, the full unmasked loss on the original images.
4 Conclusion
We introduced ELIPPS, a self-supervised training paradigm for undersampled inverse problems that
requires neither ground-truth images nor fully sampled measurements: training uses only
undersampled inputs together with incomplete measurements on a fixed supervision set. Two
structural properties render this seemingly ill-posed problem well-posed, invariance of the data
distribution under a family of transformations and intertwining of these transformations with the
forward operator. Over the induced equivariant class the partially self-supervised risk is
proportional to the full self-supervised risk, equivariance can be enforced architecturally at no
cost when the initial reconstruction is a least-squares right-inverse of the subsampled forward
operator, and the minimizer over the trained architecture class is the conditional expectation,
which in the image domain is the minimum mean squared error estimator up to ker⁡(A)\ker(A). Over an
arbitrary equivariant class the two risks have the same minimizers, so misspecification causes an
approximation error but never an inconsistent training target; and uneven coverage leaves the conditional expectation identified exactly wherever the transformed supervision sets reach at all. For CT with the dihedral group D4D_{4}, supervision on one eighth of the sinogram
brings both detector upscaling and sparse-view reconstruction to the same order of accuracy as a
reference model trained on full clean measurements.
Several directions remain. The equivalence of the two risks requires only conditionally centered
supervision noise, which covers pre-log Poisson statistics exactly and the log-transformed count
model up to a quantifiable bias; the analysis of the factorized architecture, in contrast, uses an
independence structure that is exact only for i.i.d. Gaussian noise, and the FBP is not a
least-squares right-inverse. Quantitative estimates for approximate right-inverses and for
approximate independence are an open question. The conditional centering is also what forces the
two acquisitions, and blind-spot architectures achieve the same independence from a single
measurement, at the price of a network constraint that would have to be made compatible with the
equivariance imposed here. Further directions include nonlinear forward operators, learned initial
reconstructions, continuous symmetry groups, and other modalities such as magnetic resonance
imaging or photoacoustic tomography.
